# Week 5: Introduction to Muscle Structure and Function

This week introduces the fundamentals of skeletal muscle structure and function, building from the **cellular scale** (the contractile machinery, the cross-bridge cycle, calcium cycling, and the volume-fraction trade-offs that define fiber types) up to the **tissue scale** (the isometric force-length and isotonic force-velocity properties, force-power-velocity relationships, and the design trade-offs that link muscle architecture to whole-body movement). A central theme is that **trade-offs are present at every structural level** — between speed and force, between force and fatigue resistance, between range of motion and force, and between thermal sensitivity and high-frequency performance.

## Lectures

- [**Lecture 11: Introduction to Muscle Structure and Function 1 — Cellular Scale**](lecture11-muscle-cellular) (28 slides)
   Skeletal muscle terminology and ultrastructure (sarcolemma, sarcoplasm, sarcoplasmic reticulum, T-tubules, mitochondrial sub-populations); the cross-bridge cycle and the role of ATP; calcium cycling and the activation-relaxation cycle; twitch kinetics across fiber types; the **zero-sum game** of muscle volume fractions (myofibrils, SR, mitochondria) and how it produces fiber-type continua; classical fiber-type categories (I, IIA, IIX/B); training-induced shifts in mitochondrial volume density.

- [**Lecture 12: Introduction to Muscle Structure and Function 2 — Tissue Scale**](lecture12-muscle-tissue) (32 slides)
   Recap of the zero-sum game and how training breaks it (hypertrophy); hummingbird flight muscle as an extreme aerobic specialization; regional endothermy in tunas/mackerel sharks and heater organs; types of muscle contraction (concentric, eccentric, isometric, isotonic, isokinetic); the **isometric force-length** and **isotonic force-velocity** relationships and how they are measured experimentally; the 3D force-length-velocity surface; activation scaling; the force-power-velocity relationship and the existence of an optimal velocity for peak power.

- [**Week 5 Friday Review and Discussion**](friday-review) (12 slides)
   Extends the Week 5 cellular- and tissue-scale material into questions of plasticity, aging, and applied biomechanics: **satellite cells** and the cellular basis of "**muscle memory**" (myonuclear retention); **sarcopenia** and the rise of **hybrid (co-expressing) fiber types** with age; how endurance vs. resistance training each modulate fiber type and size in seniors; **muscle quantity vs. quality** (intramuscular fat infiltration) in the triceps surae; a high-heels study showing muscle–tendon plasticity and walking-economy effects; and two practice activities — constructing a **power–velocity curve** from F–V data and inferring **functional characteristics** from a volume-fraction pie chart.
