# Week 6: Integrative Muscle Structure and Function — Organ, Limb, and Whole-Organism Levels

This week extends the muscle physiology unit from the **cellular and tissue scales** of Week 5 up through the **organ (muscle–tendon)**, **limb (skeletal lever)**, and **whole-organism (locomotion)** scales. A central theme is that **trade-offs between force and displacement** appear at every structural level — within tissue intrinsic properties, in muscle–tendon architecture, and in the joint lever systems through which muscles act on the skeleton. The week culminates with **integrative experiments** that measure muscle function *in vivo* during real movement, showing how anatomy and intrinsic properties combine to produce the diverse mechanical roles muscles play in locomotion (motor, spring, brake, strut).

## Lectures

- [**Lecture 13: Integrative Muscle Structure and Function 3 — Organ and Limb Level**](lecture13-muscle-organ-limb) (37 slides)
   Recap of intrinsic tissue properties (Hill-type force–length and force–velocity, activation effects, fiber type, efficiency, sprint–fatigue trade-offs); muscle–tendon architecture (parallel vs. pennate vs. multipennate; **physiological cross-sectional area**, PCSA); the architectural force–length and force–velocity trade-off at the whole muscle–tendon unit level; tendon length and stiffness relative to muscle fiber length and PCSA; introduction to **musculoskeletal lever systems** (torque balance, moment arms, the bicep example); and the **scaling of effective mechanical advantage (EMA)** with body size in mammals and birds.

- [**Lecture 14: Integrative Muscle Function 4 — Limb and Whole-Organism Level**](lecture14-muscle-whole-organism) (37 slides)
   Inverse dynamics and the proximo-distal gradient of joint function in cursorial limbs; *in vivo* measurement of muscle dynamics with **sonomicrometry** and **tendon buckles**; **muscle work loops** as a tool for visualizing energy flow; classic case studies of muscle–tendon function during locomotion (turkey lateral gastrocnemius, wallaby plantaris, guinea fowl gastrocnemius, cockatiel pectoralis); the **flow-of-energy framework** (motor, spring, brake) for understanding muscle–tendon function; *in vivo* ultrasound measurements in human walking and running; and applications of these principles to **prosthetic limbs** and **assistive exoskeletons**.
